# Engine review for the 30 Sep go/no-go

Written 28 Sep 2026 for the launch decision (launch, gated soft launch, or
push). It covers the verdict rules as they now stand, the evidence for each
threshold, what changed on the golden set and on the repositories people
actually looked up on githolt.com, 10 more hand checks, and what is still
wrong.

## In one screen

- **Hand checks: 10 of 10 agree** (was 1 of 10 when the engine work started,
  8 of 10 on main before this change). Flask is now "Not worth"; request
  (deprecated) is now "Not worth".
- **Busy repos are read properly.** Rates now count only pull requests (PRs)
  that are at least two weeks old, and Holt reads further back on a busy
  repository to find enough of them. Before, pytorch's 200 newest PRs covered
  two days and the rates were built from the fast outcomes only.
- **New rules**: a 5% merge-rate floor, "inactive" (no merge and no push
  in 90 days), a minimum of 10 outside merges before calling a project a
  rubber stamp, and (decided 28 Sep) **slow replies are a note, not a
  reason**: a project that merges outside work is "Worth your time" with
  "Replies are slow here: typically N days, beyond your D-day budget."
- **The budget is the reader's**: the report page asks "How long can you
  wait for a first reply?" (1 week / 2 weeks / 1 month). Changing it never
  changes the verdict, only the slow note, and is served from the same
  GitHub read (no second fetch).
- **Prod re-run** (304 repos, fresh from GitHub): Worth 88% → 84%, Not enough
  7% → 12%, Not worth 5% → 4%; 63 of 304 changed. 24 "Worth" reports carry
  the slow note at 1 week (9 at 2 weeks, 1 at a month).
- **My 10 extra hand checks** on student favourites: 9 of 10 agree.
- **Cost**: a busy repository costs up to 8 more GitHub points (10 → at most
  18) and roughly 5–40 seconds more (median 33 s → 51 s). About half of
  prod repos (147 of 304) need the extra read.
- **Recommendation**: the engine is ready for the **gated soft launch** on
  1 Oct (friends and testers). Both open calls from the first version of
  this pack are decided and built: slow replies → "Worth" with a note, and
  the extra read on busy repositories stays as is.

## 1. The rules, in plain English

Holt reads a repository's recent PRs and counts what happened to the ones
from **outside contributors**: people who aren't on the project's team. The
team is everyone GitHub shows as an owner, member or collaborator, plus anyone
the history shows merging PRs (their own included, since that needs write
access), closing other people's, or formally reviewing 3 or more. Bots don't
count. Drafts and PRs labelled as spam or invalid are left out.

**Only PRs opened at least 14 days ago count.** Newer ones are listed but not
counted, whatever has happened to them so far.

Then the first rule that applies gives the answer:

| # | Rule | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Archived | Not worth |
| 2 | A mirror or fork; the work happens elsewhere | Not worth |
| 3 | **New.** Nothing merged and nothing pushed in 90 days | Not worth |
| 4 | A catalogue or list (most outside PRs add one entry or line) | Not worth |
| 5 | Nobody outside opened a PR we can count | Not enough evidence |
| 6 | None merged, and over 70% of at least 8 got no reply at all | Not worth |
| 7 | 2+ merged, from 2+ **different people who got merged** | Worth, unless 8 or 9. **New:** if the typical first reply takes longer than your budget, a note says so |
| 8 | …but over 60% merged and under 20% of **outside** merges got any comment from a person, over **at least 10** outside merges | Not worth |
| 9 | **New.** …but under 5% of outside PRs were merged | Not worth |
| 10 | Otherwise (too few merges, all merges from one person); slow replies are said beside the reason | Not enough evidence |

A model never changes any of this; in AI reports it only explains.

### Why each threshold

**Only PRs two weeks old (the settled sample).** On the 52-repo golden set,
20 repositories' newest 200 PRs covered less than 14 days. Counting the young
PRs that already had an outcome inflated merge rates, because the ones with
an outcome after two days are the quick merges:

| repo | rate over young "decided" PRs (main) | over PRs 2+ weeks old |
|---|---|---|
| openssl | 40% | 11% (on the old recording) |
| react-native | 76% | 42% |
| TheAlgorithms/Python | 70% | 62% |

Two weeks is the window ticket 06 chose (a holiday or release freeze, not a
month of silence); it is unchanged.

**Reading further back on busy repos.** When the newest 200 PRs hold fewer
than 60 outside PRs that are two weeks old, the live fetch searches for PRs
opened before the window, 25 at a time, until it has 60, and stops after 8
extra pages. 60 because the provider can only guess who is on the team (the
engine later removes staff who read as CONTRIBUTOR), and pytorch at a target
of 40 ended with only 27 countable PRs. Measured cost is in section 5.

**The 5% floor.** Before it, enough merges from enough people passed however
many attempts they came from: flask merged 5 of 171 outside PRs and read
"Worth". On the golden set plus the first 155 prod repos read (the full set
is below the table), the floor would turn these down:

| floor | turned down |
|---|---|
| 5% | flask (2.9%), chatwoot (3.6%, 82% no reply), oppia (3.7%) |
| 10% | adds git/git (9.6%), gemini-cli (9.7%) |
| 15% | adds bat, cockroach, zulip, llvm, winforms, sway |
| 20% | adds CircuitVerse, aspnetcore, gatsby, ruff, divkit |

5% sits in a clear gap and only catches repositories where outside work
almost never lands. 10% already turns down git (whose GitGitGadget path
does land outside work) and 15% turns down zulip, a GSoC organisation known
for mentoring newcomers. So: 5%. The audit asked for "at least 40 attempts";
that is built in: with 2 merges needed to pass at all, a rate under 5% means
at least 41 attempts. On all 344 repositories (golden plus prod, fresh data),
5% turns down 5: flask, chatwoot, oppia, nginx and Rocket.Chat. 10% would add
6 more, including django (8.2%, hand-checked "Worth" in section 4) and git.

**No-reply share: it does not gate "Worth".** Among repositories that are
otherwise "Worth", golang/go has 56% of outside PRs with no reply at all,
and it is a hand-checked "Worth": most Go contributions go through Gerrit,
and the GitHub PR is a mirror nobody answers. Over all 344 repositories, a 50% gate would turn 19 "Worth"
repositories down, Go and vscode among them; a 70% gate would catch two
(bat, 82% unanswered, 11% merged; tidb, 87%, 5%). The share stays visible in the report ("82% got no reply") and in
the web's odds line, and it still decides when nothing was merged (rule 6).

**Rubber stamp: 10 outside merges, outside merges only.** The rule used to
count every merge, including maintainers merging their own work unreviewed.
plantuml read "Not worth" because its owner merges his own commits without
comment (19% reviewed overall), while 47% of outside contributors' merges got
a comment. Counting outside merges only fixes that. The minimum of 10: every
repository the rule fires on today has 13 or more outside merges, so the
minimum changes nothing now; it stops "0 of 3 merges got a comment" from
turning a small project down.

**Slow replies (decided 28 Sep).** Once only two-week-old PRs count, first
replies look slower than before: the fast answers on days-old PRs no longer
dominate. In the first version of this pack, 25 prod repositories became
"Not enough evidence" only because the typical first reply took longer than
7 days, efcore (41 of 44 merged) and highlight.js (78 of 148) among them.
There is plenty of evidence there; the reader just has to wait. So when the
merge conditions hold, the answer is "Worth your time" and the reason line
ends "Replies are slow here: typically 11.6 days, beyond your 7-day budget."
The budget is the reader's choice (7, 14 or 30 days on the report page; the
API takes 1–90). The verdict never depends on it any more, so the server
answers another budget from the report it already has, rewriting only that
note; nothing is read from GitHub again. The frozen competition benchmark
keeps its old rule.

**Inactive.** request/request (deprecated) read "Not enough evidence": its
last merge was in February 2020. When nothing has been merged in 90 days and
GitHub shows no push in 90 days either, the answer is now "Not worth" with
that sentence. A recent push without merges keeps the old informational line
("work may be landing some other way"), so projects that land work off
GitHub aren't hit.

## 5. Cost of reading busy repositories

Each GraphQL page of 25 PRs costs 1 point; a normal report is 10 points
(8 pages, repository facts, docs). On the prod re-run:

| | repos | median fetch time* |
|---|---|---|
| newest 200 were enough | 126 | 33 s |
| needed the extra read | 147 (42 hit the 8-page cap) | 51 s (90th percentile 67 s) |
| fewer than 200 PRs in total | 31 | shorter |

GitHub points for all 304: 3,654 (12 per report on average; 18 at most).
*Timed with 6 reports running at once, so slower than a single report.

Measured one at a time (no other load), with the final settings:

| repo | newest 200 only | with the extra read |
|---|---|---|
| pytorch | 10 pts, 25 s | 14 pts, 46 s (300 PRs) |
| openssl | 10 pts, 37 s | 13 pts, 41 s (262 PRs) |
| llvm | 10 pts, 43 s | 14 pts, 80 s (300 PRs) |
| django | 10 pts, 24 s | no extra read needed |

So a busy repository costs 3–8 more points and roughly 5–40 seconds more.
**Approved as is (28 Sep).** If it ever needs trimming, the lever is
`SETTLED_MAX_PAGES` (8) in `src/holt/evidence/github_graphql.py`. A
different budget on the report page costs nothing: it's served from the same
read.

The screening pass (`discover`, `find`) doesn't do the extra read; it is a
pre-filter and stays fast.

## 6. Known limits (not fixed)

- **Staff who don't look like staff.** People in a company's org with private
  membership read as CONTRIBUTOR. Holt catches them when they merge, close
  others' PRs, formally review 3+ PRs, or merge their own; PostHog's staff do
  none of those in the sample (they merge through a queue bot), so their
  merges read as outside merges and PostHog is turned down as a rubber stamp.
  Right answer, wrong reason.
- **Success stories leave the outsider count.** Someone who became a
  collaborator after their first merges (Oppia, layer5) counts as team for
  their whole history, because GitHub's association is read today.
- **The budget selector is on the free report only.** On the AI tab another
  budget would be another paid run; AI reports keep the budget they were
  made with.
- **One merge keeps a mostly-ignoring project at "Not enough"** (appwrite).
- **Latency**: busy repositories take longer (section 5); the web shows the
  same progress bar.
- **The first-timer count is an upper bound** (from #87), and first-timers now
  count by merge time, so someone with two PRs open before their first merge
  counts both.
- **Screens and the find page** still use the older arithmetic without the
  two-week window (a pre-filter, as before #97).
- **The prod "before" is the 26 Sep snapshot** (304 repositories) from the
  audit, not a fresh read of the prod database: the read-only query was blocked
  in this session. The "after" is a fresh read from GitHub on 28 Sep.
- **Thresholds were chosen on the data they are shown on** (golden set plus
  prod). The gaps are wide at 5%, but there is no held-out set.
